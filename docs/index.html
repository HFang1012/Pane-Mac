Scrawl

Draw on anything.

Flick it, or click to spin

Pick a pencil, an eraser or a color picker from the wheel and mark up whatever’s on your screen. Choose the color and thickness of your lines, and how wide the eraser rubs out. Hide your drawings for a moment, bring them back, or clear them all. Keep only the tools you want on the wheel, in the order you like.

Away

Blur your screen while you’re gone.

I’m away from my computer right now. Away since 3:42 PM

Click to sip

Your screen blurs and the pointer hides, so nobody can read what you left open. A note of your own shows on every screen, with an icon like coffee, lunch or a meeting. Pick the colors of its glow, and show how long you’ve been gone. It doesn’t lock your Mac, and it works without any extra permissions.

Spin

Run your music from a wheel.

Point here to play

Play, pause, skip and set the volume from a wheel that opens right where you point. Slide out to see the songs coming up, and let go on one to play it. Works with Spotify, Apple Music, or any player through the media keys. Link Spotify once to scroll its queue and start your playlists.

Jot

Quick notes that remind you later.

Time’s up

New note

Pull it back, then let go

Write a note, pick a timer, and send it off until it’s time. When it comes back, snooze it for later or mark it done. Add a screenshot to a note, or take it off again. Drag a note by its top to move it anywhere on your screen, and see what’s coming up in Pane.

Tweak

Point at a file and edit it.

Double-tap right ⌥, then click a file

Drag a corner, or click to turn

Crop, turn and resize pictures, trim or mute a video, trim songs, and find and replace in text. Rename anything or change its type, then save over it or as a copy, with the old version going to the Trash. Point at a folder to look inside it as icons, a list, columns or a gallery. Tweak any file right from there.

A small panel opens over whatever you’re doing and scores how your Mac is holding up. See how hard the processor, memory, battery and storage are working, and which apps are behind it. Install Pane

Two ways in. The Terminal one is quicker.

1. One command Recommended

Downloads Pane, puts it in Applications and opens it.

curl -fsSL https://hfang1012.github.io/Pane-Mac/install.sh | sh
  1. Open Terminal. Press ⌘ Space, type Terminal, press Return.
  2. Paste the command and press Return. Pane opens by itself when it’s done.
  3. Turn on Pane in Accessibility when it asks (see below).
Why no security prompt? macOS only checks apps that a web browser downloaded. This command downloads with curl, so macOS opens Pane straight away. Run the same command any time to update. Had Scrawl before? It replaces the old app and keeps your drawings.

2. The regular download

Works fine, but macOS asks you to confirm once.

Pane

Applications
  1. Open Pane.dmg and drag Pane into Applications. Then eject the Pane disk.
  2. Open Pane. macOS will stop it the first time with a message like this: ! “Pane” Not Opened Apple could not verify “Pane” is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy. DoneMove to Trash Click Done. Don’t move it to the Trash.
  3. Allow it in System Settings. Go to Apple menu›System Settings›Privacy & Security. Scroll down to Security, where it says “Pane” was blocked to protect your Mac. Click Open Anyway, confirm with your password or Touch ID, then click Open Anyway once more.
  4. That’s it. Pane opens normally from now on. Each new download asks once again, which is another reason to use the command.
Why does macOS ask? Apple asks developers to pay for a yearly account to “notarize” apps. Pane is a free project and isn’t notarized, so macOS can’t vouch for it and asks you instead. The code is open for anyone to read on GitHub.

Then: allow Accessibility

Scrawl and Spin need this to notice when you hold their keys, Jot and Tweak to notice a double-tap, and Tweak to see which file is under your pointer. Away works without it.

  1. Go to System Settings›Privacy & Security›Accessibility and turn on Pane.
  2. Already on but Scrawl doesn’t react? Remove Pane with the – button and add it again. macOS treats each new version as a new app.
